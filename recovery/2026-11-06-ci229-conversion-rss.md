## Alternatives and risks

1. Preserve the real64MiB guard and reduce proven application allocation churn. The current private-row/spill-buffer improvements remain valid. A next major option is a bounded query-local small-node arena: charge complete backing/slack until physically released, preserve result/copy lifetime and cancellation, poison/unpoison recycled nodes for ASan, and test exception safety on every platform. It could reduce quarantine traffic, but std/Boost allocator propagation, escaping copied rows, strict ownership handoff and peak slack make a hurried arena unsafe. A source-level allocation site and actual before/after totals should precede that change; no guessed pool/global allocator is introduced here.
2. Obtain the missing native evidence in a separate diagnostic execution: the already-prepared one-shot quota LLDB probe for the conversion, and an owned-child RSS rejection stop with a bounded vmmap summary plus allocation/free counter deltas. A debugger/map collection alters timing/layout, so retain the unchanged cold gate separately and mark permissions/tool failures explicitly. No native run is launched in this phase and no denied artifact path is bypassed.
3. A policy decision could separate normal-build positive RSS efficiency from instrumented memory-safety/typed-RSS-rejection checks. The real guard would stay active, but accepting rejection instead of successful completion under ASan changes the positive-test contract. That is not an allocation bug fix and is not implemented without explicit approval. Raising64MiB, changing its metric, warming away cold pages or reducing sanitizer retention/checks solely for a green result is not done.
