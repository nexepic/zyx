# Stratified untimed property-supply diagnosis

`protocol.json` was frozen before the first matrix and `followup-protocol.json` before the aggregate decomposition. `observed.log` and `followup.log` retain the scoped PerfTrace observations and full oracle test results; their JSON summaries include protocol hashes. Both runs used the coverage-instrumented SCAN/HYBRID test fixture and report work counters only, not elapsed attribution. The COUNT fusion experiment was default-off. Three fixtures (128, 192 and 512 owners) and all query/gather oracle checks passed in both runs.

The initial matrix covers sequential 32-owner/two-column gathers, sparse multi-group requests, unordered duplicate restoration, old/new snapshots across a property update, and a reopened labeled filter with two aggregate properties. An event absent from a scenario means the observer did not record it, not that the operation did not occur. Gather cases within a fixture run sequentially against a pinned snapshot and may inherit caches and cursors from earlier cases; they are not independent cold-start measurements. For example, the 192-owner sparse request spans three groups but recorded two group decodes after the dense request had already touched a group.

The follow-up compared the same reopened labeled predicate with SUM alone, COUNT(property) alone, and the combined query. At 128 owners the COUNT and combined queries each recorded 128 owner-route checks and two group decodes. At 192 they each recorded 384 checks and six group decodes; at 512, 1024 checks and 16 group decodes. SUM alone recorded three and eight group decodes at 192 and 512 respectively, and did not emit the performed-owner-check event. The `MembershipCache` has two group entries and retains authenticated-owner bits only for cached groups, so the scale-dependent counts are consistent with cache reuse differences across more than two groups. This is a hypothesis, not a proven explanation: the queries use different plans and the events do not label individual phases or owners. `COUNT(property)` also has a separate non-null probe path, and any cross-stage authority reuse must preserve exact snapshot identity, competing-ROW checks, update/recovery invalidation, cancellation and bounded memory.

Decision: do not enlarge the cache, suppress checks or add a fused shortcut based on these counters. The counts identify a worthwhile next investigation: a single-query diagnostic that records bounded phase/owner/group identity without borrowing authority across snapshot or callback boundaries, with an independently frozen control and full old/new snapshot oracle. It must establish whether the same owner and same snapshot are reauthenticated and whether a bounded same-window consumer can eliminate that repetition without moving costs or increasing write/retention overhead. If it cannot, stop this candidate and collect representative real workload inputs before choosing a different supply/locating change.

The often-cited 57.17% is from `../current-boundary/all_nodes_property_filter.receipt.json` and `../measure-current-boundary.py`: the medium experimental B image, single-core-affinity diagnostic, four warmups and 80 full-oracle samples, 40 instrumented and 40 alternating controls. Observed/control P50 was 1.00754, within the preregistered 5% perturbation threshold. The 57.17% is exclusive elapsed time at the measured supply boundary after excluding its callback interval; it is not a CPU share and does not allocate that boundary among authentication, location or decode. Its data and earlier binary are not available here, and it is not the current run or an end-to-end speedup.
